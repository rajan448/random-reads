# 1. Protect your data before asking

> **TL;DR:** Share the smallest useful summary, and keep account access outside the assistant.

## Give the assistant a summary

Think of your financial records as a house. You can discuss the electricity bill without handing over every key.

Start with fictional examples. When you need personal analysis, prepare category totals yourself using a calculator or spreadsheet.

**Redaction** means removing sensitive details. Remove names, addresses, account numbers, transaction references, and identifying descriptions before sharing anything.

| Original detail | Safer substitute |
| --- | --- |
| Full statement with account identifiers | Monthly category totals |
| Employer name and salary reference | Monthly take-home income |
| Named medical provider | Health spending total |
| Specific lender and account number | Loan A, balance, rate, minimum payment |

These substitutes reduce exposure. They do not make your financial circumstances anonymous. Exact dates, unusual purchases, and combined details can still identify you.

Never paste passwords, card security codes, one-time verification codes, payment PINs, or wallet recovery phrases. Avoid uploading identity documents or complete tax returns to a general chat service.

If you must share a document excerpt, check the resulting file for searchable hidden text and extra pages. Covering text visually may leave it recoverable. Creating a fresh summary is simpler.

## Check where your information goes

As of November 2026, verify these details in your provider's current privacy policy and settings:

- **Retention:** how long conversations and uploaded files remain stored.
- **Training use:** whether your content can help improve models.
- **Memory:** whether the assistant saves details for later conversations.
- **Third-party sharing:** whether connected services receive your information.
- **Deletion:** what removal covers, including any exceptions.

Do not assume disabling training also eliminates storage or access. If you cannot understand the terms, use fictional data. The [UK National Cyber Security Centre's privacy guidance](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/blog-post/chatgpt-and-large-language-models-whats-the-risk) explains why provider terms matter before sharing sensitive information.

## Limit connected access

A **connector** links an assistant to another service, such as a bank or email account. **Read-only access** allows viewing information without changing it; it still exposes whatever the connection can read.

For your first workflow, use no bank connection. If you later connect a service, inspect its permissions, restrict the accounts and duration, and confirm how to revoke access. Enable **multi-factor authentication**, which requires an additional proof beyond your password.

A **local model** runs on your device. It can reduce external sharing only if processing stays there. Check whether the application sends files, usage records, or backups elsewhere. Local operation does not prevent incorrect answers.

## A safer first request

Instead of uploading your statement, say:

```text
Here are fictional monthly totals in rupees:
income 60,000; essential spending 36,000;
minimum debt payments 4,000; optional spending 8,000.
What information would you need to draft a budget?
Do not request identifying details or account access.
```

## Key takeaways

- Start with fictional data or category totals.
- Treat summaries as sensitive, even after removing identifiers.
- Check current privacy terms before sharing personal information.
- Keep credentials and payment permissions outside the assistant.
