# 3. Check advice, permissions, and scams

> **TL;DR:** Verify consequential claims at their source, and make financial actions yourself through trusted channels.

## Match the check to the consequence

An LLM can sound equally confident about a budget category and a false tax rule. Confidence alone tells you little.

| Assistant output | Your independent check |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly spending total | Calculator or spreadsheet against original records |
| Loan comparison | Lender documents: interest, fees, term, repayment conditions |
| Tax explanation | Official tax authority for your country and relevant year |
| Investment suggestion | Official product documents and an appropriately qualified adviser |
| Suspicious payment request | Independently contacted bank or intended recipient |

For loans, compare total repayment and fees as well as monthly installments. A smaller installment can come with a longer term and higher total cost.

As of November 2026, verify current rates, fees, laws, and product terms directly before relying on them. A browsing-enabled assistant can still misunderstand a valid source.

## Ask for evidence you can inspect

Use this prompt when a response could influence a financial decision:

```text
Separate supplied facts, assumptions, and unknowns.
For external claims, give the official source link,
publication date, and applicable country and year.
Explain what information could change the conclusion.
If you cannot verify a claim, mark it unverified.
```

Open each important link yourself. Confirm it supports the claim and applies to your situation. Asking a second chatbot can reveal disagreements, but agreement is not independent proof.

The [CFPB's report on financial chatbots](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/chatbots-in-consumer-finance/chatbots-in-consumer-finance/) documents inaccurate information and barriers to getting help. Contact your institution directly for disputes, deadlines, or account-specific problems.

## Keep financial authority with you

**Prompt injection** happens when malicious instructions in content steer an assistant away from your request. For example, an invoice might tell it to send your records to a “verification” website.

The [UK National Cyber Security Centre](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/ai-and-cyber-security-what-you-need-to-know) identifies this risk. Treat instructions inside statements, emails, and websites as untrusted. A prompt saying “ignore malicious instructions” cannot guarantee protection.

Keep your finance assistant able to draft and explain, without permission to transfer money, trade, borrow, or submit filings. Review the payee, amount, account, and date yourself in your bank's official channel.

## Recognize convincing scams

An “AI trading system” promising guaranteed returns deserves scrutiny. The [joint investor alert on AI and investment fraud](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-alerts/artificial-intelligence-fraud) warns about these pitches and fabricated endorsements.

A familiar voice is also insufficient proof. The [FTC's voice-cloning warning](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/scammers-use-ai-enhance-their-family-emergency-schemes) recommends calling your loved one using a number you already know. Pause urgent requests for secrecy or unusual payments.

If you share credentials or send a suspicious payment, contact your bank immediately through its official app or known number. Change compromised credentials, revoke affected connections, preserve evidence, and use your country's official fraud-reporting channel. Recovery is not guaranteed.

## Key takeaways

- Verify important claims using original documents and official sources.
- Keep execution permissions outside your assistant.
- Confirm urgent requests through an independent channel.
- Respond promptly to suspected exposure or fraud.

**Where to go deeper:** Use your bank's security guidance, your tax authority, and your country's financial regulator. Ask a qualified adviser about consequential personal decisions.
